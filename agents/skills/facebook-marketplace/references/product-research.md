# Product research procedure

## Purpose and inputs

Use this procedure after a provisional intake read to turn the available item
identity, seller area, condition evidence, seed links, photos, and research
access into traceable product evidence. It supports a truthful listing and a
buyer decision model; it never upgrades an unobserved, untested, or
seller-unconfirmed fact into a listing claim. Preserve the provenance terms in
the listing record: seller-confirmed, source-supported, observed, inferred, or
unknown.

Start with the strongest identity evidence available (label, model number, SKU,
manual, packaging, or seller statement). If a precise variant remains
unverified, describe the ambiguity and research the plausible variants only far
enough to identify a material difference. Do not use a product-specific
checklist: the questions, evidence, and stopping point must fit the item at
hand.

## Source matrix

Use sources for the kind of claim they are suited to support. Capture the exact
page or post consulted, not only a search-results page.

| Source tier | Strongest uses | Limits and handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OEM specifications and support | Official model identification, specifications, manuals, compatibility statements, supported features, service and warranty terms | OEM sources lead on specifications and support policy, but do not establish subjective quality, real-world reliability, or every historical bundle. Retain the exact model or region limitation. |
| Independent technical reviews | Measured or hands-on performance, trade-offs, comparative context, and practical use conditions | Prefer transparent methods and product-matched reviews. Separate the reviewer's evaluation from a specification, and note version, firmware, or sample limitations. |
| Forums and owner discussions | Recurring owner concerns, edge cases, setup pitfalls, and vocabulary buyers use | A forum claim is an attributed owner report, not a fact about this item. Look for independent corroboration; otherwise state the source and uncertainty without generalizing. |
| Retailers | Current availability, offered configurations, replacement-price context, and sometimes included-item descriptions | Inventory, sale prices, bundles, and listings can be stale or incorrect. Treat retailer content as an offer, not completed-sale evidence or proof of what the seller includes. |
| General web sources | Discovery, discontinued models, archival documentation, regional naming, and leads to stronger sources | Verify consequential claims with a higher-quality source where possible. Record when a claim remains only a lead. |

## Evidence contracts

### Identification

Match the item to a manufacturer, model, generation, SKU, and variant only to
the precision the evidence supports. Prefer a readable item label, then an OEM
manual or support page tied to that label. Compare visual cues only as an
inference and retain competing variants when they would affect compatibility,
value, or buyer expectations.

### Feature verification

Use OEM documentation for a feature's stated capability, prerequisites, and
limitations. Use independent sources when the material question is how that
capability performs in normal use. Do not infer that the seller's individual
unit has a feature enabled, works correctly, or includes a related accessory.

### Compatibility

Identify the counterpart, standard, region, firmware, operating environment,
adapter, or version that matters to the prospective buyer. State whether the
source says compatible, conditionally compatible, or unsupported; do not turn
an unverified setup into a blanket compatibility promise.

### Recurring owner concerns

Search owner discussions and independent reviews for repeated, specific
concerns that can affect a buyer's decision. Record the pattern, affected
version or conditions, number and independence of corroborating reports when
observable, and whether the concern can be checked on this unit. Preserve an
unresolved report as an attributed concern rather than presenting it as a
defect.

### Current availability and replacement-price context

Use several current retailer or official offers to establish whether the item
or substitute is still available, its typical configuration, and a replacement
price range. Record currency, region, condition (new/refurbished/open-box),
bundle, and date. This is context for valuation, not a claim that local used
items sell for that amount.

## Claim record

For every material research claim, keep a concise record in the listing
record's Markdown body or an attached research note:

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Claim ID | A stable local label for cross-reference. |
| URL | Direct source URL or preserved source identifier. |
| Source type | OEM, independent review, forum/owner discussion, retailer, or general web. |
| access date | Date the source was consulted, in YYYY-MM-DD form. |
| Supported claim | What the source actually supports, including model/variant scope. |
| confidence | High, medium, or low, with a short reason. |
| conflicts or limits | Contrary evidence, missing context, stale date, attribution, or unresolved ambiguity. |

Keep source-supported research distinct from observed condition, seller
confirmation, and inference. A source can establish that a model supports a
feature; only seller or item-specific evidence can establish that the offered
unit includes it or that it works.

## Buyer decision model

Build this model dynamically from the identified item, credible product
evidence, local market observations, and the seller's actual evidence. For the
item at hand, identify:

- What buyers compare: exact model or variant, age or generation, condition,
  included components, compatibility, fulfillment, and relevant alternatives.
- Material value drivers: the few attributes that genuinely change usefulness,
  risk, or replacement cost for this category.
- Trust signals: item-specific evidence that can be truthfully shown, such as
  model-label clarity, condition detail, included-item proof, or a stated test
  result.
- Recurring objections: buyer concerns supported by independent reviews or
  carefully attributed owner discussion, including which are relevant to this
  unit and which remain unknown.
- Technical facts needing plain-language translation: specifications whose
  practical effect is material to a purchase decision.
- Photos or tests that would reduce uncertainty: only evidence that would
  change truthful identification, valuation, safe use, or a buyer's ability to
  compare the item.

Use the model to direct research, inspection, media selection, price reasoning,
and factual copy. It is a decision aid, not a generic feature inventory.

## Plain-language translation

Preserve the exact technical fact, its scope, and its source. Add a
plain-language consequence only when the evidence supports that consequence;
otherwise describe the decision question rather than predicting an outcome.

For example: OEM documentation lists [exact specification]. In practical
terms, that means [supported consequence] when used with [stated prerequisite].
Do not convert a rated maximum into guaranteed real-world performance, a
compatibility statement into universal fit, or a model capability into a claim
about this unit's condition.

## Conflicts and uncertainty

Keep different kinds of evidence side by side when they answer different
questions. Example: an OEM page may state that a model has a water-resistance
rating under its stated test conditions, while repeated owner reports describe
failures after a particular use pattern. Record the OEM feature claim as a
specification and the owner reports as attributed, corroborated experience;
neither disproves the other, and neither establishes the condition of the item
being sold. Do not collapse the conflict into a single quality verdict.

When sources directly conflict on the same claim, prefer the source most
authoritative for that claim, preserve the disagreement in the claim record,
and avoid buyer-facing certainty until it is resolved or immaterial.

## Proportional stopping rule

Begin with identity and the buyer decision model, then add sources only while
they could materially change identification, valuation, positioning, safety or
compatibility risk, or the next evidence request. Stop when additional sources
are unlikely to change one of those decisions, and record the remaining
confidence limits. Escalate only for a material ambiguity, a safety-relevant
question, a high-value item, or a conflict that would alter truthful copy or
price.
