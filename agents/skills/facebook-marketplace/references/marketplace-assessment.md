# Facebook Marketplace competitive assessment

## Purpose and inputs

Use this procedure to compare a truthful prospective listing with the local
Marketplace environment. It consumes the best available item identity, seller
area, condition and included-item evidence, and any seed links. It produces
local competitor observations, limits on the comparison, pricing and copy
implications, and ethical factual opportunities. A comparison supports a
recommendation; it never substitutes for seller confirmation or authorizes
unsupported buyer-facing claims.

Default to browser-based local comparison when that access is available, since
location, visible listing detail, and recency can be material. If access is not
available, state that limitation, use supplied observations or other market
evidence carefully, and do not imply that an unobserved Marketplace search was
performed.

## Search and location scope

Read the project's or listing's configured seller area before searching. Record
the postal area, city, radius, or geographic scope actually used, plus the date
and any visibility limits. Do not silently assume that a default radius is the
seller's market.

Search the identified item through a compact set of variants appropriate to the
evidence:

- Exact model and exact variant where known.
- Shortened model name or number.
- Product family or generation name.
- Common abbreviations.
- Category terms that a seller might use instead of the model.
- Likely misspellings, spacing changes, and omitted punctuation.

Keep the queries and scope with the observations so another reviewer can judge
coverage. Do not search by irrelevant keyword stuffing merely to expand the
sample.

## Observation table contract

For every comparison listing, record only what is visible or reasonably
inferable. Use unknown rather than completing a field by assumption.

| Field | Record and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Listing reference | Stable link or identifier, query used, and observation date. |
| Asking price | The displayed asking price, currency, and whether it appears negotiable. This is not a sale price. |
| Visible price change | A displayed reduction, prior price, or unknown; do not infer negotiation history. |
| Condition | Seller-stated condition and visible evidence, kept distinct. |
| Exact variant | Model, generation, color, size, SKU, or unknown. |
| Included items | Explicitly listed or visible components; unshown items remain unknown. |
| Location and distance | Displayed location, distance when shown, and geographic scope used. |
| Photo strategy | Observable ordering, coverage, clarity, condition disclosure, and privacy concerns; describe facts, not a presumed motive. |
| Title strategy | Observable identification precision and relevant factual terms; do not copy wording or keyword tactics. |
| Description quality | Whether it gives material identification, condition, included items, test evidence, limitations, and handoff facts. |
| Observable recency | Displayed posting or update age, or unknown; do not equate it with availability. |
| Apparent seller type | Individual, business-like, or unknown, only when reasonably inferable from visible information. |

## Price evidence and sparse samples

Treat local Marketplace prices as asking prices unless reliable completed-sale
evidence is visibly available. Do not present an asking price as proof of value,
demand, or a completed transaction. Record completed-sale evidence separately,
with its source, date, item match, condition, and limitations; if none exists,
say so plainly.

When the sample is sparse, recover proportionally: vary the model and category
queries, widen the geographic scope in deliberate increments, and supplement
with other market evidence such as credible sold-item sources or current
replacement-price context. Preserve the limitations of every expansion: a
broader geography may not reflect local convenience, other platforms may have
different fees or buyer behavior, and a weak item match cannot establish the
value of the seller's variant.

## Comparison and competitiveness judgment

Compare like with like before drawing a price implication. Adjust the reasoning
for confirmed variant, condition evidence, tests, included items, visible wear
or defects, location and handoff convenience, and listing recency. Keep
condition evidence distinct from assertions a competing seller makes.

When the category is time-sensitive (seasonal goods, weather-driven demand,
school-year or holiday timing), record the current local demand signal with
its source and date, and let it shape the pricing direction and urgency.

State a market competitiveness judgment: whether the observed range and
listing quality leave room for a more credible or better-supported listing, or
whether more listing work is unlikely to create meaningful upside. Explain the
evidence and the limitations rather than claiming precision a small sample
cannot support.

## Ethical marketing-gap analysis

Look for gaps a seller can close truthfully with their own evidence, not tactics
to imitate. Useful gaps may include:

- Credible specificity: exact identification where competitors are vague.
- Better identification: a readable model or variant label and correctly named
  category.
- Condition proof: clear photos and honest defect disclosure.
- Testing evidence: the actual scope and result of a seller-performed test.
- Accessory clarity: an explicit, accurate included-items list.
- Objection handling: a factual answer to a recurring, item-relevant concern.
- Photo order: a clear first image followed by condition, identifiers,
  components, and limitations.
- Transaction convenience: only the pickup, delivery, shipping, or availability
  terms the seller can actually offer.

Never copy a competitor's text, photos, title phrasing, or tactics. Do not use
deceptive urgency, fake scarcity, unsupported claims, all-caps spam, hidden
defects, misleading price anchors, or competitor errors as an opportunity.

## Positive output recipe

Return a compact, evidence-labelled assessment containing:

1. **Market observations:** search scope, relevant asking-price observations,
   item matches, and meaningful comparison differences.
2. **Limitations:** sample size, access date, geographic and visibility limits,
   unresolved variants, and whether completed-sale evidence was available.
3. **Pricing implications:** a defensible range or direction that distinguishes
   local asking prices from completed-sale evidence and replacement context.
4. **Title or copy implications:** factual identification or buyer questions
   worth addressing; never copied competitor wording.
5. **Photo implications:** the seller-owned photo or test that would most
   reduce a material uncertainty.
6. **Ethical opportunities:** specific, supportable ways to reduce uncertainty
   or improve convenience, plus a judgment about whether the effort is likely
   to create meaningful upside.

Maintain citations or listing references for material observations and keep
uncertain inferences labelled. The output should help the seller compete with
clarity and evidence, not manufacture pressure or hide risk.
